section_4:
  badge: Our approach
  title: Objectives, beneficiaries and networking
  layout: acordeón
  items:
    - icon: target
      color: primary
      title: Specific objectives
      desc: 'Each child will have objectives set according to their profile, needs, strengths, interests and level of development.'
      items:
        - Strengthen communication and language skills, considering the different ways each child communicates.
        - Support the development of fine and gross motor skills, coordination, balance, motor planning and bodily participation.
        - Promote strategies for better regulation and processing of sensory information, according to individual needs.
        - Develop autonomy and personal independence skills related to activities of daily living.
        - Stimulate cognitive processes and learning skills, considering each child's level of development and learning style.
        - Strengthen social and interaction skills, especially through group activities.
        - Facilitate educational participation through support strategies and curricular adaptations when needed.
        - Provide families with guidance and practical strategies to support learning continuity at home.
        - Establish interdisciplinary coordination mechanisms with health and education professionals and other relevant fields.
        - Monitor each child's progress periodically and adjust intervention objectives as they evolve.
        - Build a support network for the child and the family.
    - icon: users
      color: secondary
      title: Who we serve
      desc: 'Direct beneficiaries: children aged 3 to 12.'
      items:
        - Have a diagnosis of ASD.
        - Have support needs related to neurodevelopment and require specialized assessment.
        - Require intervention in one or more of the developmental areas covered by the program.
        - 'Indirect beneficiaries: mothers, fathers and caregivers.'
        - 'Indirect beneficiaries: teachers and schools, through coordination when appropriate.'
